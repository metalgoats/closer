## The one piece of context that changes how you think about this

**Closer runs the same prompt Gabriel uses in ChatGPT.** The prompt is not the variable.
What ChatGPT has is months of accumulated context about him, and that is what produces the
better outputs. Ivan is building the history export separately — that work is NOT in this
round.

So: do not try to out-write the prompt from scratch. The job is to make the system
**extract more from each transcript** and **carry it forward properly**, so it needs less
accumulated context to sound right.

## The pipeline as it stands

1. **Debrief pass** — full transcript in, structured JSON out (9 sections + the GHL note,
   `EXPECTED_DEBRIEF_CHARS = 14000`).
2. **Message pass** — SMS + email, generated in **three tones in parallel** so the tone
   slider is instant. Each is fed `draftContext(call, parsed)` — a distillation of the
   debrief — **not the transcript.**

> ⚠️ **The guard, and do not "fix" it.** The message drafts are deliberately denied the
> transcript. The debrief contains coaching critique *of Gabriel*, and that has no business
> anywhere near a client-facing email. The comment in `llm.js` says so explicitly. Anything
> you need in the drafts must be **extracted in the debrief pass and passed forward through
> `draftContext`.** That constraint shapes task 1 below.

## The work, in priority order

### 1. Adaptive output selection

Gabriel narrates his own follow-up during calls: *"I'm going to send you an email, and the
email is going to have this, and this, and this."* He wants the system to read the
transcript for those cues and prepare exactly what he said he would send.

**Implementation follows the guard.** The debrief pass is the only stage that sees the
transcript, so add to the debrief output schema something like `statedFollowUps` — what
Gabriel committed to sending, in his words, with any contents he specified — then surface it
through `draftContext` into the message pass.

**Hard requirement: never suppress the SMS.** Even when he only mentioned an email:

> "I would send a text message and an email to every single client if it didn't take too
> much time. Regardless whether it's a sale or not, it's always good to end on a good note,
> even if they decide not to buy."

He skips it today because it is more work than it is worth. Removing that friction is the
product.

### 2. Recipient profile

Gabriel made this call himself, and it is a product decision, not a preference:

> "I don't even know if I necessarily want it to sound like me. I want it to sound like
> exactly what that person needs to hear, based on what came up in the conversation."

Build a profile of **the client** from the language they used on the call — how they talk,
what they care about, what they disclosed — and shape the follow-up to how that person best
receives things. Same pattern: extract in the debrief pass, carry through `draftContext`.

His reason is also the marketing copy:

> "I get the notes back and I'm like, I had no idea that they were a doctor, or I had no
> idea that they had a sister… they say these things, but it's important to them, and it
> literally doesn't even register in my mind."

### 3. Rebuild the GoHighLevel note

His spec, close to verbatim: **more bulleted, more spaced out, concise, easy to read.**
Enough for **him or anyone else on the team** to understand:

- the client
- the goals
- the outcomes
- what was said on the call
- possible selling points
- possible pitfalls and things to look out for

> ⚠️ **There is a format spec that beats anything you would invent.** The `GAB sales` folder
> in Gabriel's OpenAI account holds months of reports generated with this prompt, showing
> exactly the format and feedback style he wants. **It is not exported yet.** If it is not
> available when you get here, do the structural work and leave the wording for a second
> pass rather than guessing.

### 4. Collapse the three simultaneous outputs

Front-end, in `public/`. Ivan's observation from watching him work: he does not send the
email, SMS and CRM note together — sometimes one, then the next, sometimes neither. They
should not all hold screen real estate at once. Keep the dashboard feel.

## How to verify — this is not optional

**Generate a real debrief and read the output.** Reading the code is not verification, and
this project has an expensive history of exactly that mistake. `npm run dev` with seeded
data, run a generation, open the result and read it as Gabriel would.

Specifically check: the SMS still generates when the transcript only mentions an email; the
GHL note is genuinely scannable; and **no coaching critique of Gabriel has leaked into a
client-facing draft.** That last one is a release blocker.

Also worth knowing: real transcripts ran ~19k tokens against an original 7k estimate
(~2.7× low). Re-baseline any cost estimate against logged numbers.
